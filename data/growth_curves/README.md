## Experimental methods

Transcribed from the workbook's `Readme` sheet, with details confirmed by e-mail
(thread "Re: Plan for CUE results", October–November 2026):

- **Pre-culture.** MIT1002 grown in rich medium (½ YTSS: yeast extract, tryptone,
  artificial seawater) for ~4 h, to early exponential phase.
- **Washing.** Cells pelleted, washed three times in, and resuspended in,
  carbon-free minimal medium.
- **Medium.** Minimal Basal Medium (MBM; recipe in `../media_sources/`) with L1
  trace element solution added, plus one carbon source.
- **Carbon.** 6, 8 or 12 mM carbon (`carbon_mM_C`; e.g. 2 mM glucose = 12 mM C).
  Two substrates — mannuronic acid and glycerol 3-phosphate — were added as a few
  crystals, too little to weigh, dissolved in ~1 mL of medium and
  filter-sterilized. Their `carbon_mM_C` is empty.
- **Inoculum.** 1% of total volume.
- **Incubation and measurement.** 28 °C with constant shaking in a BioTek Synergy
  H1 plate reader, in 48- or 96-well plates, OD600 read hourly for up to 72 h.
- **Replicates.** 3 or 4 wells per substrate and concentration, each separately
  inoculated.
- **Blank.** OD600 of **uninoculated** medium was subtracted from every reading
  (confirmed by Christa Smith, 2026-11). Values near zero, or slightly negative,
  are expected where there was no growth.

### No inoculated no-carbon control

There is no well with cells but no added carbon. The question such a control
answers — could carbon carried over from the rich pre-culture support growth? —
is answered by the substrates that did not grow: alginate, allantoin, formate,
glycolate, xylose and others were inoculated the same way and rose by ≤ 0.015.
Carryover after three washes and a 1% inoculum is also too small to explain
increases of 0.05 or more, when glucose at 12 mM C reaches about 0.2.

## Processing (raw → tidy)

All in `code/growth_curves/clean_growth_curves.py`, and repeated in the CSV
header:

1. Reshaped to one row per well per time point.
2. Split each label (`12C_Acetate`) into `carbon_mM_C` and `substrate`.
3. Numbered replicate wells 1…n within each substrate and concentration, in
   sheet order.
4. Fixed one spelling: `Glucuronic aid` → `Glucuronic acid`. All other substrate
   names are as written by the lab, including the three distinct glycerol
   phosphate reagents: `Glycerol 3-phosphate` (unknown amount),
   `(rac)-sn-Glycerol 3-phosphate` and `rac-Glycerol 1-phosphate`.
5. The last sheet row has no time value. Readings are hourly and the row before
   it is 72 h, so it is set to 73 h. Only four substrates have a reading there.
6. Dropped empty cells: runs were of different lengths (24–73 h).
7. Rounded OD600 to 3 decimal places, the instrument's precision, removing
   floating-point residue left by the blank subtraction.

## Things to know before using these data

- **Runs.** Judging by where each set of wells stops (24, 47, 48, 49, 72/73 h),
  the sheet combines about five separate plate runs. The run is not recorded in
  the sheet. The 24 h run (glucose, acetate, galactose, fructose, sucrose,
  mannuronic acid) has no non-growing substrate in it.
- **Short runs.** Some curves are still rising when the run ends (e.g. lysine,
  DHPS, 12 mM C glycerol 1-phosphate), so their final increase is a lower bound.
- **Disagreements with earlier calls.** Sucrose, fructose, choline and ectoine
  grow here although `known_growth_phenotypes.tsv` lists them as `No`;
  6-phosphogluconate and phosphoenolpyruvate grow only weakly, at 12 mM C,
  although they are listed as `Yes`. Growth calls from these curves are made in
  `code/growth_curves/` and are not yet copied into the phenotype table.
